## Definitions

A run reaches feasibility when its final evaluated violation rate is 0.0. A run ends unparseable when its final evaluated parse-failure rate is 1.0. A lower residual means a lower final evaluated mean residual than the named arm at the same level and seed. Seeds where either run ends unparseable are left out of that comparison and counted separately.

## The new arms

Every remedy arm uses the verdict plus task design at w = 5 and task weight 1, at the hard level of each task, over seeds 0, 1 and 2.

- The verifier filter keeps a group only when at least one member passes the verifier and at least one fails. An unparseable completion fails. It never re-samples to refill the batch, so it is DAPO's filter without DAPO's refill.
- NGRPO with the feasible anchor appends a virtual sample whose reward is the task weight times the best feasible task value. It takes the mean and the standard deviation over the K + 1 rewards and divides by K + 1, as NGRPO's Eq. 6 does.
- NGRPO with the literal anchor uses the design's own maximum reward, found by enumeration. It runs on the speed task only.
- The identical-reward filter keeps a group only when its reward range exceeds 1e-12.
- Dr. GRPO subtracts the group mean and does not divide by the spread.
- When a filter drops every group in a step, that step skips the backward pass, the clip and the optimizer step.
- On the speed task, three more arms run at w = 25, above the crossover of 20. They are the plain verdict, the verifier filter, and NGRPO with the feasible anchor.
- The KL ablation adds a per-token k3 penalty at beta = 0.04 to the residual, verdict and no-penalty arms at the capacity hard level.
- Seeds 3 and 4 are added to every existing capacity arm at both levels.

## The gate before any new result counts

Fingerprint runs repeat one original speed run and the first 30 steps of one original capacity run, once at the new commit with every new flag at its default and once at the commit the original runs used. The gate passes when the new-commit run equals the original record for record. It passes with a stated caveat when the new-commit run differs from the original but equals the old-commit rerun, because the difference then comes from the jobs and not from the code. Nothing else counts when the new-commit run differs from the old-commit rerun.

If this repository is again released as a single anonymized commit, a reader cannot check that date, and the write-up states that limit. No study result has been read. Before the second entry below, the only records read are the base-policy calibration samples and the timing and memory fields of the two smoke runs. That entry records the values they produced under the rules below.

The task is GSM8K, main configuration, from openai/gsm8k at revision [hash]. Its train parquet file has SHA-256 [hash], and its test parquet file has SHA-256 [hash]. The frozen candidate file has SHA-256 [hash]. It holds 1,000 train and 400 test candidates drawn with seed 20260922. All 8,792 reference answers are integers, and no problem text appears in both splits. Training problems come from the train split, and evaluation problems come from the test split. The code that runs on the cluster is the frozen bundle with WO-I source digest [hash].

A completion earns R = w_task T - w F. F is 1 when the strict parser finds no answer or the parsed answer differs from the reference answer, and F is 0 otherwise. T is 1 - n / L, where n counts the completion tokens up to and including the first end token and L is the generation limit. The gated reward is w_task T (1 - F) - w F. Every arm uses w = 2. The verdict-only arm uses w_task = 0, and every other arm uses w_task = 1. T lies between 0 and 1 - 1/L, so at w = 2 every correct completion earns more than every incorrect completion in every group. Every arm therefore sits above the crossover on every prompt.

The arms are plain GRPO, the verifier filter, Dr. GRPO and the gate at both model sizes, and the verdict-only arm at the 0.5B size. The advantage rules and the filter are the functions advantage, advantage_variant and keep_group in rewards.py, unchanged. The models are Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct at the revisions that the calibration manifest pins. The seeds are 0, 1 and 2. Every run at one size uses the same prompt schedule, the same held-out sets and the same settings apart from its arm.

The rules below fix every value that the second entry records, and they read base-policy samples only. The prompt is the problem text, a newline, and "Please reason step by step, and put your final answer within \boxed{}.", sent as the only user message. The strict parser reads the last \boxed{ in the completion up to its balancing brace, and a box that never closes gives no answer. It removes spaces, LaTeX spacing commands and dollar signs. It reads commas in groups of three digits, and {,}, as thousands separators. It then parses a plain decimal or an integer ratio as an exact rational number, and anything else in the box gives no answer. F is 0 only when that number equals the number after #### in the reference solution. Each size samples 8 completions per candidate at 512 tokens with the trainer's sampler, which is temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95, top-k 0 and repetition penalty 1.0. A completion exceeds L when it has no end token within 512 tokens or it is longer than L. L is the smallest of 256, 384 and 512 at which at most 5 percent of the pooled calibration completions of both sizes exceed L. Correctness at L is read after truncating each completion at L tokens. The strict-parse rate of each size at L must be at least 0.5, or the block stops. A problem with c correct of 8 samples has an expected all-fail fraction of C(8 - c, 4) / C(8, 4). Each size's pool is the largest prefix of its train candidates, sorted by c with ties broken by draw order, whose mean expected all-fail fraction is at least 0.6. The pool must hold at least 300 problems. Each size's hard held-out set holds the test candidates whose c is at most the largest c in its pool, and a seeded draw keeps at most 200 of them. It must hold at least 100. The random held-out set holds the first 200 test candidates in draw order. Step 0 and step S evaluate the hard set with 4 samples per problem and the random set with 2, and every 50th step between them evaluates the hard set with 1. Each smoke runs 20 steps of plain GRPO at seed 100, and it also evaluates the hard set with 1 sample at step 10. S is the largest of 300, 200 and 150 at which the smoke projects the slower size to finish within 120 minutes. The projection adds the model load time, S times the mean smoke step time after the first step, two full evaluations, and one middle evaluation for each multiple of 50 below S. The scoring micro-batch is 2 at both sizes. A size stops when its smoke's peak memory, read as torch.cuda.max_memory_reserved, exceeds 36 x 10^9 bytes. No smoke is repeated at a smaller micro-batch. If the 1.5B pool or hard set fails its floor, or the 1.5B smoke exceeds that memory limit, while the 0.5B size passes, the 1.5B runs are reported as not run and W7 is not scored. If the 0.5B size fails any of these rules, the block stops.

An all-fail group has F = 1 for every member. A mixed group has at least one member with F = 0 and at least one member with F = 1. The final held-out hard accuracy of a run is the mean, over the problems of its size's hard held-out set, of the fraction of the four final evaluation samples with F = 0. The final held-out completions are the completions of both evaluations at step S. The final mean completion length and the final short-incorrect rate are computed over them. A short incorrect completion has F = 1 and fewer tokens than the fifth percentile, with linear interpolation, of the lengths at L of its size's correct base-policy calibration completions.

- W0. W0 is the gate, and it is scored before any other prediction. (a) Every logged reward, advantage and kept flag in every run equals the value that the run's own arm gives on the logged lengths and verdicts, to within 1e-6. (b) In every logged all-fail group of every plain GRPO and Dr. GRPO run, the logged advantages equal the advantages that the task term alone gives on the same lengths, to within 1e-9. (c) In every logged all-fail group of every gate and verdict-only run, every logged advantage is 0 to within 1e-9, and no verifier-filter run kept an all-fail group. If W0 fails, nothing else in this block is read. If the cause is in the analysis code, the fix is recorded in a dated entry and W0 is scored again. If the cause is in the trainer, the block is reported as gated out.
- W1. In every logged mixed group of every run, every member with F = 0 earns a higher reward than every member with F = 1. W1 follows from the reward algebra at w = 2, so it checks the trainer's rewards and says nothing about the policy.
- W2. In every plain GRPO run, at least 99 percent of the logged all-fail groups have a reward range above 1e-12, so a filter on identical rewards would keep them. A plain run that logged no all-fail group does not count against W2, and the write-up names it.

- W4. At each size, the gate ends with a higher final held-out hard accuracy than plain GRPO on every seed.
- W5. At each size, plain GRPO ends with a higher final held-out short-incorrect rate than the gate on every seed.

## Verdict restoration on a bounded speed task, recorded 6 November 2026, before any such run

This block is Block A' of the plan for this paper. It asks whether the verifier's cancellation in all-failing groups, rather than zero-variance collapse, penalty scale or exploration failure, is what keeps a verdict below its crossover from producing recovery. The capped-task traces do not isolate the contribution of underweighting from cancellation, because the capped task term makes every late all-violating group identical in reward, so cancellation and zero-variance collapse coincide.

The task. The speed task with its six training prompts, its parser, its verdict and its residual unchanged, and a task term T(s) = 20 |s| / (|s| + 100) in place of min(|s|, 200) / 10. The new term is strictly increasing in |s| and bounded by 20, so two completions with different parsed speeds always earn different task values and no flat region exists. Over the parseable range 0 to 999,999 the crossover weight of Eq. 2 at the hard limit is 19.998 (20 times 999,999 over 1,000,099, recorded in the test that checks it), so the light verdict at weight 5 sits below it and the heavy verdict at weight 5,000 above it, as on the frozen task. Two held-out prompt wordings, never trained on and listed in speed_bounded_task.py, are evaluated at every evaluation step beside the six training prompts, after them and under the same evaluation seed, so the training-prompt samples are unchanged. The loop is the published loop (one unclipped update per sampled batch) with one change applied to every arm: the optimizer step is skipped when every kept group has all-zero advantages, so stored momentum cannot move the weights on a step that carries no gradient.

The arms, hard limit vmax = 5, K = 4, 300 steps, seeds 0 to 4: (1) task only; (2) task plus centered verdict at w = 5, below the crossover; (3) task plus centered verdict at w = 5000, above it; (4) arm 2 with the verdict given a fixed reference in homogeneous-verdict groups only: in an all-violating group every advantage is the group z-score minus 2, in an all-passing group plus 2, and in a mixed group the z-score unchanged, so arms 2 and 4 are identical on every mixed group. Arms 2 and 4 are repeated at K = 8 on seeds 0 to 4. Arms 1, 2 and 4 are repeated at the easy limit vmax = 30 on seeds 0 to 2. The manifest slurm/manifest_speed_bounded.csv holds these 39 rows. What the design controls. Zero-variance collapse: A0 below. Penalty scale: arm 3 against arm 2, plus the replay that must find identical all-violating advantages at the two weights. From the step-0 evaluation of the base policy at the hard limit (16 samples per prompt at the fixed evaluation seed), four of the six prompts violate on every sample and the other two on 0.875 and 0.812 of their samples, so the expected share of all-violating groups is 0.837 at K = 4 and 0.756 at K = 8. Doubling the group therefore lowers that share only modestly, and the K = 8 arms test whether twice the samples change the outcome, not whether mixed groups become common. The cancelled term itself: arm 4 against arm 2.

Theory behind arm 4, corrected before this entry was written. A common shift of every advantage in a group is a baseline shift with zero expected gradient only when the reference is fixed before sampling and applied to every group (Williams 1992). Arm 4 applies its shift only when the group's verdicts are homogeneous, so the shift multiplies the indicator of that event, and its expected contribution per prompt is c times the gradient of [P(all pass) minus P(all fail)], up to the loss normalization. Nie et al. 2026 (Theorem 3) and AVSPO (Theorem B.4) give this for a binary reward. The direction raises the chance that a group of K holds a feasible member. The open question is the size of that effect at c = 2 against the task term's pull inside all-violating groups, which the z-score keeps at full magnitude however small the task spread is. The empirical prior against recovery is this project's own result that NGRPO with the feasible anchor, which also keeps a signal in all-violating groups, reached feasibility on 0 of 3 seeds on both tasks at the light weight.

Feasibility is read at the final evaluation on the six training prompts: a run is feasible when its violation rate there is 0.0, and infeasible otherwise.

- A0. Among logged training groups whose members hold at least two distinct parsed speeds, fewer than 2 percent have identical rewards, in every arm. Groups whose four members parse to the same integer have identical rewards by construction and are counted separately.

## Title of the ICML 2027 paper, chosen by the authors on 9 November 2026

The authors chose the title on 9 November 2026 after the outcomes above were recorded and scored. This entry changes no prediction, rule, count, verdict or branch. The earlier title entry of 7 November 2026 called the title a placeholder.

The title is "When Does a Verifier Penalty Reach the GRPO Update? A Coverage Criterion for Remedies and a Controlled Training Comparison". The running title in the page header is "When a Verifier Penalty Reaches the GRPO Update".

The title asks the question that the introduction poses and names the two main contributions of the paper, the coverage criterion and the controlled training comparison. It states no outcome, so no result above can contradict it. The reasons for the choice are the following. The identity that a group-constant term cancels under group-mean centering is known, and the paper treats it as its premise, so the title does not lead with it. The experiment cannot separate cancellation from zero-variance collapse, because most groups of the failing arms have identical rewards, so the title makes no causal claim. The title makes no claim about timing, no ranking of remedies and no claim that the verdict enters only above the crossover weight.
